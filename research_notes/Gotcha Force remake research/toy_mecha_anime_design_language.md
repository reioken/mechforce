# Visual Design Language of Capsule-Toy Mecha, Toy Mecha Lines, and Kid-Hero Mecha Anime (Reference for a Gotcha Force Spiritual Successor)

Scope note: These notes cover (1) gashapon/capsule-toy culture and the collectible hooks of adjacent toy lines, (2) SD mecha proportions and hero/grunt/fantasy-fusion design codes, (3) how to build a large, readable roster, (4) combiner design, (5) anime cast design for a kid-hero mecha show (2000s vs modern Trigger-style), and (6) villain/kaiju design. Each "Cited Findings" bullet carries its source. "Inferences" hold synthesized, actionable recommendations for a roster of 50+ chunky "capsule mechs" built procedurally from primitives (boxes, cylinders, spheres, cones, extrusions) with cel-shading. ## 1. Gashapon/capsule-toy culture and collectible toy lines: how do they work, and what makes them collectible?

### Takeaway
Gashapon is a blind-purchase, series-based format (¥100–500 per crank, 65–75 mm capsules). What makes it collectible is a lineup shown on the machine's display card, hidden "secret" figures (about 1 in 100) that often aren't shown on the card, material and color variants (clear, metallic, alternate plastic colors), fast rotation (Bandai retires products after 1–2 months), and duplicates that push people to trade. Each adjacent toy line added one strong hook a capsule-mech game can reuse: modular parts (Medabots, LEGO CCBS), elemental color factions (Bakugan, Duel Masters, Bionicle, Beyblade), a ball that pops open into a creature (Bakugan), and rare-material chase pieces (Bionicle chrome and gold masks, clear/metallic gashapon secrets).

### Cited Findings

**Mechanics, history, business**
- The name is onomatopoeia: "gasha" is the sound of the hand crank and "pon" is the capsule landing in the tray. Price range is ¥100–500 per capsule. Toys are typically PVC with detailed molding and paint. — [Wikipedia: Gashapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gashapon)
- Capsule vending machines started in the US in the 1880s (gumballs in New York). They reached Japan in 1965 and spread through the 1970s. Bandai has expanded gashapon since 1977 and holds "Gashapon" as a registered trademark in Japan, China, the US, the EU and the UK. Ryuzo Shigeta is credited with setting up Japan's first machine and is called the "Grandfather of Gacha". — [Wikipedia: Gashapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gashapon)
- As of March 2021, Bandai Namco had sold 3.711 billion Gashapon toys at ¥100–500 each since 1977. Early products included erasers based on Kinnikuman, Super Deformed Gundam and kaiju. Specialty gashapon stores with dozens to over 100 machines appeared in the 2000s, and adult collectors are a growing segment. — [Wikipedia: Gashapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gashapon)
- Blind purchase: buyers can't choose items, so collectors often trade duplicates to complete sets. — [Wikipedia: Gashapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gashapon)
- Modern pulls cost ¥200–500. Capsules are typically 65 mm or 75 mm in diameter. Bandai runs "500+ active series". Secret/hidden figures have an "~1:100 pull rate". Variant types listed: standard, secret/hidden, clear or metallic paint variants, and convention exclusives. Series size varies widely, e.g. Cat Life has 8 variants and Demon Slayer has 36. Retired series resell at "5–20x the original machine price". — [Gashapoint Capsule Toy Encyclopedia](https://gashapoint.com/capsule-toys/) (aggregator; treat numbers as approximate)
- Gashapoint claims Bandai Namco earned "over ¥90 billion (~$600M USD) in annual revenue as of 2023" from capsule toys. — [Gashapoint](https://gashapoint.com/capsule-toys/) (unverified aggregator figure)
- Display cards are cardboard inserts on the front of each machine that advertise the lineup inside. — [Flickr: Gashapon Display Cards album](https://www.flickr.com/photos/kinggoji/albums/72157638513315666/). Sets often include "secret rares" that are highly collectible and not always shown on the display sheet. — [DFW Gacha Guide: The Gachapon Secrets](https://dfwgachaguide.com/gachapon-secrets/) (search excerpt)
- **Bandai primary source (Integrated Report 2024):**
  - Bandai releases over 1,200 GASHAPON products a year. Products rotate out 1–2 months after release, and popular items may come back about 6 months later.
  - The team's stated goal is "to ensure that we create capsule toy products that will appeal to fans and that offer fun and excitement when opened". Priorities are "new specifications, new materials, and new IPs", and products must be "exciting, fun, and eye-catching".
  - The audience has moved from mainly children to "people of all ages, primarily adult women".
  - Sales data collected from the machines themselves guide development.
  - CAPCHARA products dispense with no capsule at all.
  — [Bandai Namco Holdings IR: Developing the Capsule Toy Gashapon That Offers Excitement When Opened](https://www.bandainamco.co.jp/en/ir/library/feature05_02_2024.html)
- Bandai Namco has collected and recycled empty capsules since 2006, about 42 tons a year. — [Bandai Namco Amusement: eco initiatives](https://bandainamco-am.co.jp/en/others/capsule-toy-store/topics/20250331_topics02.html) (search excerpt)

**Collector psychology and regulation (relevant if any randomized purchasing exists)**
- "Kompu gacha" (collect a full set of random items to unlock a grand prize) was ruled a violation of Japan's Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations by the Consumer Affairs Agency in May 2012 (made official May 18). — [Game Developer: Why "Kompu Gacha" Was Banned](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/why-quot-kompu-gacha-quot-was-banned); [Monolith Law Office](https://monolith.law/en/general-corporate/game-random-complete-illegal); [Serkan Toto: regulation official](https://www.serkantoto.com/2012/05/18/gacha-regulation-official/)
- The concern was gambling exposure for minors. Publicized cases included a middle-school boy who spent $5,000 in a month and a younger student who spent $1,500 in three days. GREE and DeNA shares fell more than 20% in two days on the rumors. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/why-quot-kompu-gacha-quot-was-banned); [Serkan Toto: kompu gacha history](https://www.serkantoto.com/2012/05/09/kompu-gacha-dena-gree-history/); [Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/life/2012/05/16/digital/japans-social-gaming-industry-hindered-by-governments-anti-gambling-move/) (search excerpts)

**Key lines and the hook each one contributes**
- **Kinkeshi / M.U.S.C.L.E.:**
  - Bandai's Kinnikuman "keshi" (eraser-style) figures started in 1983. By the time they faded in 1988, Bandai had made 418 unique figures.
  - The line was licensed to Mattel in 1985 as "M.U.S.C.L.E." (Millions of Unusual Small Creatures Lurking Everywhere!), with 236 figures officially released in the US.
  - The first US shipment was entirely "bubble-gum pink", which collectors call "flesh". The second was half flesh and half dark blue, red or purple, so plastic color itself became a variant axis. Japanese figures came in more shades and a softer material.
  — [Wikipedia: Kinkeshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinkeshi); [Mental Floss: A Brief History of M.U.S.C.L.E. Figures](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/31625/brief-history-muscle-figures) (search excerpts)
- **SD Gundam / "Gashapon Senshi":** In 1985 Bandai launched the PVC capsule-figure line *Gashapon Senshi Series: SD Gundam World* (popularly "Gankeshi"). The figures were one color, cost ¥100 per capsule, and had "pencil-mounting leg holes that made them easy to play with", which helped drive a late-1980s boom. SD Gundam was "sometimes said to rival the 'mainline' Gundam brand in popularity" then. — [Wikipedia: SD Gundam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD_Gundam)
- **Gunpla / SD model kits:** Gundam model robots earned Bandai ¥42.8 billion in 2004. — [Wikipedia: Mecha anime and manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecha_anime_and_manga). The BB Senshi SD kit series reached its 300th kit by 2007. — [Wikipedia: SD Gundam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD_Gundam)
- **Super-robot die-cast:** By 1977 the super-robot toy market had expanded with the Chogokin die-cast series and Shogun Warriors. — [Wikipedia: Mecha anime and manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecha_anime_and_manga)
- **Zoids (Tomy, 1983):**
  - A 1/72 articulated plastic kit line based on animals, dinosaurs and insects, combined with weaponry and machinery. Wind-up or battery motors make them walk, flap wings or move their jaws. — [Wikipedia: Zoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoids); [OhEpic: Zoids history](https://ohepic.com/zoids-from-mechabonica-origins-to-iconic-mecha-toys/) (search excerpts)

  - A Medabot is assembled from four parts (head, two arms, a pair of legs) on a "Tinpet" skeleton, plus a Medal (its core/personality). — [Medabots Wiki: Medaparts](https://medabots.fandom.com/wiki/Medaparts) (search excerpt)

Flight legs are fast and evasive with low armor, best in barren areas and worst near water. Float legs are multi-terrain with high (but below Tank) armor. Multi-legged parts are fragile. — [Medabots Wiki: Leg types](https://medabots.fandom.com/wiki/Leg_types) (search excerpt)

### Inferences
- **The collectability formula, taken from the sources above:**
  1. A visible, numbered lineup (the display card).
  2. At least one hidden slot (a secret shown as "?" or not shown at all).
  3. Cheap one-color commons (Gankeshi/Kinkeshi precedent), then painted versions, then special-material versions (clear, metallic, chrome, gold).
  4. Short series windows with occasional re-releases (Bandai's 1–2 month cycle and ~6-month re-release).
  5. Duplicates that feed trading or crafting.
  6. A satisfying opening moment. Bandai's own design goal is "excitement when opened", so the capsule twist, crack and pop should be the most polished animation in the game.
- **In-game capsule station, modeled on a real machine:**
  - Each "series/wave" gets a display card listing 6–12 numbered silhouettes plus one "?" slot.
  - Keep capsule exteriors uniform within a series to preserve blind-box surprise, and reveal rarity at the opening (light color, burst, sound).
  - Use a printed rarity-symbol ladder like Pokémon's (● common, ◆ uncommon, ★ rare, ★★ super rare, ★★★ secret) on the in-game "capsule insert paper".
- **Gotcha Force's "collect capsule parts to assemble a rare Borg" is structurally a kompu-gacha loop.** It is fine as a free, earn-in-play mechanic. Do not attach real-money randomization to "complete a set to unlock" in any Japan-facing release. This is an inference from the 2012 CAA ruling; get a legal review.
- **Hooks that map directly onto a procedural primitive pipeline:**
  - **Medabots (head / L-arm / R-arm / legs + core medal) and LEGO CCBS (bones + shells + accessory bars):** these are the ideal data model for procedural mechs. Build a skeleton ("Tinpet/bones"), then snap primitive "shells" (armor boxes, domes, cones) onto sockets, then attach accessories (weapons, fins, backpacks).
  - **Medabots' seven leg types:** a ready-made locomotion taxonomy (biped, multi-leg, wheels/vehicle, treads/tank, hover/float, flight, diving) that also varies silhouettes and suits distinct arenas.
  - **Bakugan's sphere-that-opens:** the natural "capsule deploy" animation. The capsule halves become shoulder pads or a backpack, or the mech unfolds out of the ball.
  - **Zoids:** the clear canopy and chrome pilot are cheap, readable "premium" details. A transparent-material dome on the head or chest with a tiny chrome figure inside works well as a mid-rarity upgrade.
  - **Element keys (Bakugan 6, Duel Masters 5, Bionicle 6, Beyblade 4 symbols):** kids learn a fixed color-to-element-to-icon mapping instantly. Use one for faction/element coding (see Question 3).

- Bandai doesn't publish official secret-figure odds. The "~1:100" figure comes from an aggregator (Gashapoint), and I couldn't verify a typical "6–12 items per series" count from a primary source.

## 2. SD (super-deformed) mecha proportions and design rules; super robot vs real robot; Knight/Musha Gundam fantasy fusion

### Takeaway
SD mecha are about 2 heads tall (the head is 1/3–1/2 of total height), and the reason is practical. Tiny capsule PVC figures couldn't hold full mobile-suit detail, so the head and face were enlarged to carry the character's identity. The most reusable visual code in mecha is hero vs grunt:
- **Hero:** twin eyes, V-fin, faceplate, bright tricolor.
- **Grunt/villain:** mono-eye, plain or drab colors, gas-mask or blank face.
- **Toy-driven rules:** stand firmly, read by silhouette, avoid excessive lines (Okawara).

SD fantasy lines (Musha = samurai, Knight = European knight/sorcerer with JRPG influence, Sangokuden = Three Kingdoms, Command = modern military) show that any archetype can be layered onto one base chassis by swapping helmet crest, shoulder armor and signature weapon.

### Cited Findings
- SD Gundam has "an unprecedented extreme deformation, with a head-to-body ratio of roughly two", i.e. overall height equals about two heads. — [Media Mix Wiki: SD Gundam](https://mediamix.fandom.com/wiki/SD_Gundam) (search excerpt)
- An average anime character is about seven to eight heads tall. A super-deformed character's head is normally one third to one half of its height. — [Wikipedia: Chibi (style)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibi_(style)) (search excerpt)
- **Why the head is big:** "Since the size of the Gashapon PVC dolls at the time was not enough to reproduce the detailed design of mobile suits, the head was enlarged to make the character's face more clearly recognizable." SD designs were first sold as small eraser-style figures in the 1985 gashapon series SD Gundam World. — [Media Mix Wiki: SD Gundam](https://mediamix.fandom.com/wiki/SD_Gundam) (search excerpt)
- **Origin:** student Koji Yokoi submitted SD illustrations to Bandai's hobby magazine *Mokei Jōhō* ("Model Information"). Editor Satoshi Katō had him serialize SD four-panel comics, and through that he met Bandai's capsule-toy staff, which "catalyzed the 'super deformed' product concept". — [Wikipedia: SD Gundam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD_Gundam)
- **Sub-lines:** — [Wikipedia: SD Gundam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD_Gundam)
  - **BB Senshi** (1987–): the main SD kit line. "BB" originally meant the kits came with BB-pellet air guns, later removed for safety.
  - **Musha Gundam / SD Sengokuden:** Gundams as samurai, ninja and feudal warriors. The original design is credited to Koichi Yamato and Yasui Hisashi, and it is the longest-running SD series.
  - **Knight Gundam / SD Gundam Gaiden:** Gundams as knights, sorcerers and medieval European fantasy characters, designed by Koji Yokoi and incorporating "elements from Japanese role playing games, such as the Dragon Quest series".
  - **Sangokuden:** *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* with Gundams in place of the classical figures.
  - **Command Gundam:** modern-military styling.
- **Musha Gundam history:**
  - It first appeared in *Plamo Kyōshirō*, then became a 1987 SD Gundam gashapon item and in 1988 BB Senshi No. 17, "extremely popular with school children". — [Gunpla Wiki: BB Senshi Musha Gundam (1988)](https://gunpla.fandom.com/wiki/BB_Senshi_Musha_Gundam_(1988)); [Toy People News](https://www.toy-people.com/en/?p=106196) (search excerpts)
  - SD Sengokuden (1988–1992) is set in the Sengoku period and recasts mobile suits as "seven invincible samurai". — same sources.
  - The modern Legend BB Musha Gundam has a kabuto-style helmet and comes with a naginata, a katana and a Tanegashima (matchlock) rifle. — [La Scale Model: Legend BB #373 Musha Gundam](https://lascalemodel.com/products/sd-gundam-legend-bb-senshi-373-musha-gundam) (search excerpt)
- **Hero template (RX-78-2):**
  - Tomino conceived the concept and Kunio Okawara refined it into a samurai-influenced design for 1979.
  - The tricolor blue/red/white scheme came from commercial pressure. The original concept was "a grayscale machine, made up of mostly white and light gray colouring", but the studio (Sunrise) rejected it in favor of brighter colors like other super-robot shows.
  - Signature elements are the V-fin antenna (inspired by samurai helmets), a faceplate with vents that read as a mouth, and twin eyes. The V-fin and dual eyes became the template for protagonist Gundams.
  — [Wikipedia: Gundam (fictional robot)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gundam_(fictional_robot))
- **Grunt/villain template (Zaku):**
  - Okawara designed the Zaku II except for its single eye, which was Tomino's idea.
  - Early Universal Century shows followed the rule "Heroes = Dual-Eyes/Visor, Villains = Mono-Eyes", and the Zaku's single eye tells the viewer "it is the enemy".
  - People instinctively look to the eyes first.
  — [Zimmerit: The MS-06 Zaku II: An Enduring Eye-Con](https://www.zimmerit.moe/the-ms-06-zaku-ii-an-enduring-eye-con/); [Mecha Talk: Visor inferior, mono-eye superior?](https://www.mechatalk.net/viewtopic.php?t=7106) (search excerpts)
- **Real robots** tend to look plainer than super robots, with simple and/or drab colors and heads that are "either blank, cycloptic, or resemble a gas mask". — [TV Tropes: Real Robot Genre](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/RealRobotGenre) (search excerpt)
- **Super robot vs real robot (Wikipedia):** — [Wikipedia: Mecha anime and manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecha_anime_and_manga)
  - Super robots are "superhero-like", often "one-of-a-kind", run on "mystical or exotic energy sources" with "quasi-magical" abilities, and are commanded by voice or neural link.
  - Real robots are "often mass-produced on a large scale for use in wars". They use scaled-up infantry weapons (beams, firearms, swords, axes, shields), need maintenance and break down.
  - Go Nagai's *Mazinger Z* (1972) defined the super-robot genre, and Tomino's *Mobile Suit Gundam* (1979) established the real-robot genre.
  - Wikipedia credits Kunio Okawara as a "founding father" of real-robot design and Shōji Kawamori with pioneering transforming mecha (Diaclone, 1980; Macross, 1982).
- **Kunio Okawara (2024 Animedia interview, fan translation):** — [Zeonic Republic: Interview with Kunio Okawara (Animedia, 2024)](https://zeonic-republic.net/?page_id=11205)
  - Toys "were the main focus, they always required designs that could stand firmly and appeal to children with their silhouettes".
  - "Too many lines would become a huge pain for those who have to draw them over and over again."
  - "My designs are inherently three-dimensional."
  - "I've always tried to avoid grotesque designs, striving for something enjoyable for children."
- Okawara also had to work out how machines would transform and combine as toys, built mock-ups for sponsor presentations, and used 3D models to explain "Transformation" and "Union" to toy sponsors. — [UNIQLO Today: Kunio Okawara](https://www.uniqlo.com/us/en/news/topics/2025041801/); [Leaders Online: Kunio Okawara](https://leaders-online.jp/english/2536) (search excerpts; the UNIQLO page returned 503)
- **Shoji Kawamori** makes a rough sketch, builds the mecha from LEGO to work out the transformation, photographs it and traces the photo. His reason: "if you only draw sketches, you can cheat the transformation". — [Full Frontal: 40 Years of Shoji Kawamori](https://fullfrontal.moe/kawamori-40-interview/); [Mech9: Shoji Kawamori at Satelight](https://www.mech9.com/2009/10/shoji-kawamori-at-satelight.html) (search excerpts; attribution between these pages uncertain)
- **Gurren Lagann's Gunmen** (designed by Hiroyuki Imaishi and Yoh Yoshinari) are boxy robots whose torso is one giant face. The face mirrors the pilot's expressions and moves its mouth when the pilot talks. — [Gurren Lagann Wiki: Gunmen](https://gurrenlagann.fandom.com/wiki/Gunmen); [TV Tropes: Gurren Lagann Mecha](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/TengenToppaGurrenLagannMecha) (search excerpts)

### Inferences
**SD proportion rules for a "capsule mech" (standard size).** These values are recommendations derived from the ~2-head ratio and the reasoning behind it:
- **Head:** 40–50% of total height. It carries identity through the eyes, face type and crest, the same reason SD was invented.
- **Torso:** 20–25%. Often a chunky box or trapezoid with a chest vent or emblem.
- **Legs:** 25–35%. Short and thick, with oversized feet (wide boxes) so the figure "stands firmly" (Okawara).
- **Arms:** short cylinders with sphere joints and oversized mitten hands or fists (sphere or rounded box). The shoulders are the widest point after the head.
- **Silhouette check:** reduce every design to a flat black shape at thumbnail size. If it isn't recognizable, enlarge its unique feature (crest, weapon, backpack) rather than adding detail.

**Head kit: face type is the main personality switch.**

| Face type | Reads as | Primitive recipe |
|---|---|---|
| Twin eyes + V-fin + faceplate | Hero / protagonist | Box or rounded-box helmet; two emissive wedge eyes; two thin tapered extruded triangles (V-fin) from a central crest gem (small sphere or box); faceplate = small box with 2–3 horizontal slot cuts or decals |
| Mono-eye on a rail | Grunt, villain, mass-produced | Dome (half-sphere) or bucket helmet; dark horizontal slot (thin box) with a single emissive sphere; optional "gas-mask" cylinder pipes (Zaku) |
| Visor bar | Sniper, tech, cool rival, neutral | One emissive horizontal bar (thin box) or goggle lens |
| Giant face on torso (Gunmen) | Brawler, comic, monster, "boss face" | Torso = the head: big box or sphere with eyes, brows (wedges) and a grille mouth (stacked boxes); the tiny real head is optional |
| Animal head | Beast, dino, dragon, insect | Cone or box snout, sphere eyes, cone horns or mandibles (Zoids-like) |

**Other part families and primitives:**
- **Shoulder armor:** half-cylinder "barrel" pauldrons for sturdy types, domes for friendly types, cone spikes for villains, stacked flat slabs (sode) for samurai.
- **Backpacks/thrusters:** a box pack plus two cylinders with cone nozzles; wings are flat extruded polygons; a halo is a torus.
- **Weapon silhouettes** (each should read at thumbnail size):
  - Sword: long thin box with a triangle tip.
  - Beam saber: cylinder hilt with an emissive capsule blade.
  - Rifle: box with a cylinder barrel.
  - Bazooka: fat cylinder.
  - Hammer: cylinder handle with a box or cylinder head.
  - Lance/drill: long cone.
  - Shield: extruded pentagon, kite or hexagon with an emblem decal.
  - Gatling: a ring of small cylinders.

**Color codes:**
- Hero: the "tricolor" (white base with blue, red and yellow accents), following the RX-78-2 precedent.
- Grunt: one desaturated mid-value body color (military green or gray) with one accent and a pink/red mono-eye glow.
- Keep line count and panel detail low (Okawara). For cel-shading, big clean panels and a few decal lines read better than dense greebles.

**Super robot vs real robot as two design "modes" in one roster:**
- **Super mode:** bright primaries, chest emblem (animal face, star, gem), horns or crest, named finishing moves, unique one-offs.
- **Real mode:** muted colors, squad numbers, visor or mono-eye, practical weapons, mass-produced "variants of a base chassis". The real mode is ideal for procedural recoloring and swapping in a capsule game.

**Fantasy-fusion overlays** (one base chassis, swap three things: crest, shoulders, weapon):

| Overlay | Crest / helmet | Shoulders / body | Weapon |
|---|---|---|---|
| Musha / samurai | Kabuto with a large crescent or horn crest (kuwagata-style); flared neck guard | Stacked sode plates; waist tassets (skirt plates) | Katana, naginata, tanegashima rifle (all attested for Musha Gundam) |
| Ninja | Hood or cowl with visor slit or mono-eye; scarf ribbon | Minimal armor for a thin, fast silhouette | Shuriken (star extrusion), kunai, kusarigama |
| Knight (Knight Gundam) | Bucket helm with plume (cone or ribbon) or a V-fin crown | Round pauldrons, cape (flat plane) | Kite shield with emblem, lance (cone), broadsword |
| Sorcerer / wizard (Knight Gundam also had sorcerers) | Pointed hat (cone) or hood | Robe skirt (cone frustum) | Staff with orb (sphere) or floating runes (torus rings) |
| Three Kingdoms (Sangokuden) | Ornate helmet with feathers or long pheasant plumes | Big collars, layered lamellar | Guandao, spear, fan |
| Command / military | Helmet with goggles | Webbing, backpack | Rifle, grenade |

**Transform/combine feasibility:** Kawamori's LEGO rule ("you can cheat the transformation" in drawings) means any transforming or combining capsule mech should be prototyped as actual moving primitive rigs before it's committed to the roster.

### Gaps
- I found no official Bandai "SD design spec" with measured ratios. The "~2 heads" and "1/3–1/2 head" figures come from wiki and encyclopedia sources.
- I found no Hajime Katoki interview material, and the Kawamori and Okawara process quotes came via search excerpts.
- I didn't retrieve Knight Gundam-specific armor or weapon details beyond the general "knights, sorcerers, JRPG influence" description.

## 3. Designing a large, varied roster: silhouette, shape language, color coding, archetype coverage, size classes, rarity materials

### Takeaway
A roster of 50+ stays readable if:
- every unit passes a silhouette-only test (the TF2 standard);
- each unit's dominant shape matches its personality (circle = friendly, square = sturdy, triangle = aggressive or dangerous);
- colors follow a fixed element/faction key (the 5–6 element systems of Bakugan, Duel Masters and Bionicle) plus Sentai-style role colors;
- locomotion types are spread across the roster (Medabots' seven leg types);
- rarity is carried by material (one-color plastic, then paint, metallic, clear, chrome/gold, holographic/glow) rather than new geometry.

Gotcha Force's ~20 "tribes" of similarly styled Borgs is the direct precedent for organizing the roster into families.

### Cited Findings
- **Shape language:** circles read as friendly, soft and safe; squares as stable, reliable and strong; triangles as dynamic, dangerous, unpredictable and cunning ("the triangle chef seems the most threatening"). — [CGWire: Character Shape Language](https://blog.cg-wire.com/character-shape-language/); [21 Draw: Shape Language](https://www.21-draw.com/shape-language-for-strong-character-design/); [Dream Farm Studios: Shape language](https://dreamfarmstudios.com/blog/shape-language-in-character-design/) (search excerpts)
- **Team Fortress 2 (Valve, NPAR 2007):** — [Official TF2 Wiki: Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://wiki.teamfortress.com/wiki/Illustrative_Rendering_in_Team_Fortress_2); [Mitchell, Francke & Eng, "Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2" (PDF)](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf)
  - Classes have "distinct silhouettes that can be easily identified even with no lighting cues", achieved through "body proportions, weapons, and silhouette lines".
  - "Muted colours dominat[e]" with "small areas of saturation used to provide further visual interest".
  - The RED team uses "warm colours, wooden materials, and angular geometry"; BLU uses "cool colours, industrial materials, and orthogonal forms".
  - Shading "scale[s], bias[es], and warp[s] the Lambertian term" (a toon ramp) and adds a "dedicated rim term" so characters stay distinct even far from lights.
- The TF2 paper emphasizes silhouettes with rim highlights rather than dark outlines, and uses variation in luminance and hue so players can "read" the scene. — [ResearchGate: Illustrative rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262410611_Illustrative_rendering_in_Team_Fortress_2) (search excerpt)
- **Super Sentai color roles:** — [TV Tropes: Super Sentai](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/SuperSentai); [Henshin Grid: Super Sentai Color Personalities](http://henshingrid.blogspot.com/2009/04/super-sentai-color-personalities.html) (search excerpts)

  - Blue is "The Lancer", a lone wolf or foil, usually the serious one.
  - Yellow is "The Big Guy".
  - Pink (or White) tends to be the feminine one.

  - Duel Masters' five civilizations, each with a frame color and icon.

  - Bionicle's six color-coded Toa.

  - Gashapon: clear or metallic variants and secret figures. — [Gashapoint](https://gashapoint.com/capsule-toys/)
  - Bionicle: chrome silver, gold, copper and a solid-gold grand prize. — [BIONICLE Wiki: Hau](https://bionicle.fandom.com/wiki/Hau)

### Inferences
**Roster architecture for 50+ units** (Gotcha Force averaged ~10 Borgs per tribe; a smaller game should go wider and shallower):
- 12 tribes × 4 units = 48 core, plus 6 secret/chase units, plus 2–3 combiner sets. That totals about 60 distinct silhouettes before recolors.
- Within a tribe, share one "tribe trait" (crest style, weapon family or color). Vary size class, locomotion and dominant shape between members so no two units in a tribe share a silhouette.
- Recolors and material variants (see the rarity ladder below) multiply collectible count without new modeling. That's how the Kinkeshi/M.U.S.C.L.E. color variants and Bionicle's chrome and gold masks worked.

**Silhouette differentiation checklist (apply to every unit):**
1. It is identifiable as a flat black shape at 64 px tall (TF2 standard).
2. It has one "signature protrusion": a crest, a giant weapon, wings, tail, drill, cannon or cape.
3. Its dominant shape is clear: circle, square or triangle.
4. Its locomotion type is distinct from its tribe-mates.
5. Its head/face type is chosen deliberately (hero twin-eye, mono-eye, visor, animal, or face-torso).
6. Maximum saturation is reserved for small focal areas (eyes, chest emblem, weapon edge), following TF2's "muted colours dominating and small areas of saturation".

**Shape-to-personality mapping for the roster:**
- **Circle-dominant** (spheres, domes, capsules, tori): mascots, healers, idols, food bots, pets, magical girls, "cute" commons.
- **Square-dominant** (boxes, slabs, cylinders on end): tanks, fortresses, knights, construction bots, loyal big guys.
- **Triangle-dominant** (cones, wedges, spikes, fins): ninjas, jets, snipers, rivals, dragons, villains, demons.
- **Mixed:** heroes typically square body + triangle accents (V-fin, fins) + circle eyes/joints, which reads as sturdy, dynamic and friendly.

**What translates well to procedural primitives with cel-shading:**
- **Good:** big clean volumes, few panel lines (Okawara), flat colors shared between character and mech (Promare), emissive eyes and seams, oversized weapons, crests and fins as extruded 2D shapes, rim light for silhouette (TF2), decals for faces and emblems.

## 4. Combiner design: how combining robots split into component units and how the combined form reads

### Takeaway
Combiners fall into a few templates:
- **Stacked:** Getter Robo's three jets, where stacking order changes the combined form.
- **Leader-torso + four limbs:** Voltron/GoLion's radial pattern, Power Rangers Megazords, and Transformers' Scramble City with interchangeable limbs.
- **Six-unit gestalt:** Devastator.
- **Core robot + armor/support units:** GaoGaiGar's Final Fusion, Gridman + Assist Weapons.

The combined form reads as one robot when the leader unit supplies the head, torso and face, the limb units come in symmetric pairs, and the combination feels dramatic and markedly more powerful than its parts.

### Cited Findings
- **Getter Robo** was the first combining mecha. Three jets: one forms the head and arms, one the midsection, one the waist and legs. Which jet leads determines which of three robots forms (air, land, water), giving four configurations counting the separate fighters. — [Tropedia: Combining Mecha](https://tropedia.fandom.com/wiki/Combining_Mecha); [All The Tropes: Combining Mecha](https://allthetropes.org/wiki/Combining_Mecha) (search excerpts)
- **Other early combiners:** — same sources
  - GoLion (Lion Voltron) codified the radial component pattern.
  - Dairugger XV (Vehicle Voltron) used fifteen vehicles.
  - Combattler V was an early five-component combiner.
  - Zambot 3 combined an aircraft, a tank and a ground support vehicle.
- **Mighty Morphin Megazord:** — [RangerWiki: Dino Megazord](https://powerrangers.fandom.com/wiki/Dino_Megazord); [RoboWiki: Mighty Morphin' Megazord](https://robots.fandom.com/wiki/Mighty_Morphin'_Megazord) (search excerpts)
  - Tyrannosaurus (Red, leader) forms the head, torso and waist.
  - Mastodon (Black) forms the arms, and its head becomes a shield.
  - Pterodactyl (Pink) adds chest armor.
  - Triceratops (Blue) forms the left leg and Sabertooth Tiger (Yellow) the right leg.
- **GaoGaiGar (Final Fusion):** — [Brave Saga Wiki: Final Fusion](https://bravesaga.fandom.com/wiki/Final_Fusion); [GaoGaiGar Wiki: GaoGaiGar](https://gaogaigar.fandom.com/wiki/GaoGaiGar) (search excerpts)
  - Cyborg Guy fuses with the lion mech Galeon to form GaiGar, the central robot.
  - Drill Gao docks as the lower legs.
  - Liner Gao docks through the arm joints to form both shoulders and upper arms.
  - Stealth Gao lands behind the arms and forms the backpack, forearms (from its turbines), the helmet, and an extra mane for Galeon's head.
- **Transformers Scramble City combiners:** — [TFWiki: Combiner](https://tfwiki.net/wiki/Combiner); [Wikipedia: Transformers: Scramble City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformers:_Scramble_City) (search excerpts)
  - The team leader is larger and forms the torso; four smaller members form the limbs.
  - Limbs are fully interchangeable, even between teams (at least on the toys).
  - The original teams were Aerialbots/Superion, Protectobots/Defensor, Stunticons/Menasor and Combaticons/Bruticus, later joined by Technobots/Computron and Terrorcons/Abominus.
  - This torso-plus-four-limbs design differed from Takara's earlier six-robot Devastator.
- **SSSS.Gridman:** Gridman can equip "Assist Weapons" (formed by the students), and "Full Powered Gridman" is his ultimate form, combining with all four Assist Weapons at once. — [TV Tropes: SSSS.Gridman Characters](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/SSSSGridman) (search excerpt)
- **Combiner design principles (mecha design blog):** — [Aaltomies: Mecha design; Combiner Core](https://aaltomies.wordpress.com/2017/04/21/mecha-design-combiner-core/)
  - Three styles: vehicle combiners (favored by Super Sentai), humanoid combiners that become appendages (Transformers), and non-humanoid gestalts. There is also "equip-combination", where one mecha gains equipment without becoming a new entity.
  - "A combination that has no tension behind it, no emphasize or meaning, lacks impact."
  - Combiners should feel significantly stronger than their parts (Devastator as the example).
- **Gurren Lagann:** combined Gunmen stack faces, and a combined Gunmen's power is described as scaling with its number of faces. — [TV Tropes: Gurren Lagann Mecha](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/TengenToppaGurrenLagannMecha); [Gurren Lagann Wiki: Gunmen](https://gurrenlagann.fandom.com/wiki/Gunmen) (search excerpts)
- **Toy-driven origins:** Okawara had to design how machines would "transform and combine as toys" and brought mock-ups to sponsor presentations. — [UNIQLO Today](https://www.uniqlo.com/us/en/news/topics/2025041801/) (search excerpt). Kawamori prototypes transformations in LEGO because drawings let you "cheat". — [Full Frontal](https://fullfrontal.moe/kawamori-40-interview/) (search excerpt)

### Inferences
**Four combiner templates for a procedural system:**
1. **Triple Stack (Getter-type):** units A, B and C slot as head+arms, torso, and waist+legs. Reordering gives three combined forms (e.g., air, land, sea). Cheap to author, since each unit only needs "top/middle/bottom" sockets.
2. **Leader + 4 (Megazord / Voltron / Scramble City):**
   - The leader supplies the head, torso and face.
   - Two arm units and two leg units each fold into one limb shape.
   - An optional fifth "armor" unit becomes the chest plate or backpack.
   - Standardized sockets make limbs interchangeable (the Scramble City gimmick), which adds a "mix your own combiner" collectible layer.
3. **Core + Armor (Final Fusion / Full Powered Gridman):** the hero stays a complete robot, and 3–4 support units add legs, shoulders/arms, backpack and helmet. This template is the best fit for a game: the hero is playable solo, the combined form is a timed "super mode", and support units are separate capsule collectibles.
4. **Gestalt Beast (non-humanoid):** units combine into a dragon, lion or kaiju. Good for boss encounters.

**Which piece becomes what, by pattern:**

| Pattern | Head | Torso | Arms | Legs | Extra |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Getter | Unit 1 (with arms) | Unit 2 | Unit 1 | Unit 3 | Order swaps forms |
| Megazord (MMPR) | Leader (T-rex) | Leader | 5th member (Mastodon) | Two members (Triceratops, Sabertooth) | Pterodactyl = chest plate; Mastodon head = shield |
| GaoGaiGar | GaiGar (+ Stealth Gao helmet) | GaiGar (Galeon) | Liner Gao (shoulders/upper arms) + Stealth Gao (forearms) | Drill Gao (lower legs) | Stealth Gao backpack + mane |
| Scramble City | Leader | Leader | Any 2 members | Any 2 members | Interchangeable limbs |

**Readability rules for the combined form:**
- **The face belongs to the leader or hero.** Its eye style (twin eyes + V-fin) should appear only on the combined head.
- **Put a chest emblem on the core.** An animal face (a lion, as Galeon's mane implies) or the leader's emblem signals the heart of the robot.
- **Keep it symmetric.** Paired limbs should mirror each other in silhouette even if their colors differ, and the Megazord's differently colored legs still read as a pair.
- **Unify colors through:**
  - (a) the leader's color dominating the head and torso (the Megazord's red Tyrannosaurus body);
  - (b) one shared trim color (gold, silver or white) on every unit;
  - (c) one shared emissive color for all eyes and energy lines;
  - (d) shared joint/frame color (dark gray) so limbs look like one system.
- **Proportions shift when combining.** Components are SD (about 2 heads), but the combined form should use more heroic proportions (head about 1/4–1/5 of height) so it reads as bigger and "grown-up", following the size-class logic in Question 3.
- **Hide "kibble"** (leftover vehicle parts) by turning it into shoulder pads, wings, backpack or skirt armor, not stray protrusions.
- **Earn the combine** (Aaltomies): gate it behind a meter or event, use a signature camera sequence, and make the combined unit markedly stronger.

- TV Tropes' Combining Mecha analysis page was blocked (403), so the pattern taxonomy relies on Tropedia/All The Tropes excerpts and a design blog.
- I found no designer interview specifically about color unification in combiners; the unification rules above are inferences.
- Getter Robo's air date wasn't confirmed in any page I fetched.

## 5. Anime character design for a kid-hero mecha anime cast (archetypes, silhouettes, color schemes, costumes, expressions; 2000s Saturday-morning vs modern Trigger/Gridman)

### Takeaway
Kid-hero toy anime rely on stock, color-coded roles:
- a hot-blooded "red" hero vs an aloof "blue" rival (Red Oni/Blue Oni);
- Sentai-style color roles for the rest of the team;
- a signature prop that marks the protagonist (Digimon's goggles, passed between series leads);
- hair as the primary silhouette identifier.

Modern Trigger work adds flat colors shared across 2D and 3D, simplified designs built for CG, and deliberate restraint in character features. It also treats mecha and kaiju as physical suits or toys. SSSS.Gridman is the closest thematic precedent: its antagonist sculpts the toy-like kaiju that come to life.

### Cited Findings
- **Red Oni, Blue Oni:** — [TV Tropes: Red Oni, Blue Oni](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/RedOniBlueOni); [All The Tropes: Red Oni, Blue Oni/Anime and Manga](https://allthetropes.org/wiki/Red_Oni,_Blue_Oni/Anime_and_Manga) (search excerpts)
  - Red is emotional and "Hot-Blooded", associated with passion, wildness and defiance. Blue is calmer and "thinks before acting".
  - The "Stock Shōnen Hero vs Stock Shōnen Rival" pairing is the purest version: red is the "spirited, carefree, compassionate Idiot Hero who believes in The Power of Friendship", blue the "aloof, pessimistic" loner.
  - Pokémon's Ash (red) vs Gary (blue) is a cited example. The two can be close friends or bitter rivals.
- **Signature prop:** a trademark of Tai's design in Digimon is his goggles, which carry over to the lead characters of later entries. Daisuke receives Tai's goggles, and Takato also wears goggles. The leads' names even share an opening sound. — [Wikipedia: Tai Kamiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Kamiya); [Wikimon: Goggles](https://wikimon.net/Goggles); [Wikimon: Motomiya Daisuke](https://wikimon.net/Motomiya_Daisuke) (search excerpts)
- **Sentai color roles** (red leader/hero, blue lancer/lone wolf, yellow big guy, pink feminine, green goofball or smart guy): see Question 3. — [TV Tropes: Super Sentai](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/SuperSentai)
- **Hair as identifier:**
  - Because many anime characters share similar eye shapes and proportions, hair works as a primary marker of individuality and silhouette (e.g., Sailor Moon's pigtails in flight). A distinctive hairstyle stands out in branding and merchandise.
  - Common color readings: white = wisdom, age or the supernatural; red = intensity; pastels = innocence; blonde = "chosen one" or foreign.
  - Exaggerated hair is often linked to manga's need for legibility in small print.
  — [CBR: Anime characters whose hairstyles reflect personalities](https://www.cbr.com/anime-characters-hairstyles-personalities/); [TV Tropes: Anime Hair](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AnimeHair); [SKDesu: Hair in anime](https://skdesu.com/en/hair-in-anime-colors-and-hairstyles-and-their-meanings/) (search excerpts; low-authority, blog-level sources, so treat these as conventions rather than rules)
- **SSSS.Gridman / Dynazenon (director Akira Amemiya, Sakuga Blog interview):** — [Sakuga Blog: Akira Amemiya interview](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2021/05/31/ssss-gridman-and-ssss-dynazenon-interview-series-director-akira-amemiya-on-the-franchises-production-and-style/)
  - "Rigid objects, such as costumes and suits, are ill-suited for hand-drawn animation", so the tokusatsu parts were done in 3DCG (by Graphinica) to make viewers perceive the suits as physical objects.
  - Articulation was deliberately limited: "we didn't want them to move in ways that you couldn't while wearing a suit".
  - Kaiju drawn by hand "tend to come out with strong character expressions". Using 3DCG kept them reading as environmental threats rather than characters.
  - Character designer Masaru Sakamoto worked under Amemiya's restrictions: avoid distinguishing features like "twintails", and carry scenes through subtle details such as "the size of their mouths" and shifting gazes.
- **Gridman staff and kaiju:**
  - SSSS.Gridman's kaiju designers included veterans Shinji Nishikawa (Godzilla series), Hiroshi Maruyama (Ultraman series), Ichirō Itano, Osamu Yamaguchi and Mahiro Maeda. — [Easter's Kaiju Kompendium: SSSS.Gridman's Monsters](https://kaijukompendium.design.blog/ssss-gridmans-monsters/); [Gridman Wiki: SSSS.GRIDMAN](https://gridman.fandom.com/wiki/SSSS.GRIDMAN) (search excerpts)
  - In the story, antagonist Akane is a talented sculptor who makes all the show's kaiju, paralleling how Ippei Baba designed the original show's Assist Weapons. Her kaiju combine elements of original *Gridman the Hyper Agent* kaiju and Ultra kaiju, and she keeps a display case of Ultra kaiju sculpts. — [TV Tropes: SSSS.Gridman Characters](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/SSSSGridman) (search excerpt)
- **Promare (Trigger):**
  - Promare used flat colors in both its 2D and 3D sections to fix "the common problem in anime where the 2-D and 3-D stylization doesn't match". Designer Shigeto Koyama said they had tried something similar in *Panty & Stocking with Garterbelt*. — [Ani-Gamers](https://anigamers.com/interviews/promare-creating-kaleidoscopic-visual-style-trigger-hiroyuki-imaishi-shigeto-koyama-hiromi-wakabayashi/)
  - The team riffed on a flame gradient from Pixar animator Grant Alexander's concept art until they arrived at the film's pink, purple and green tones. They experimented with colored line art, black line art and lineless art before settling on the film's line style. Imaishi "had not been able to do a lot of mechanical designs since Tengen Toppa Gurren Lagann", and Promare let him release "pent-up design ideas". — [Anime Herald: Insight into PROMARE](https://www.animeherald.com/2020/05/04/insight-into-promare-from-studio-triggers-wakabayashi-and-koyama/)
  - Koyama is quoted as saying: "We tried to avoid using a typical color for it and went with pink and green to make it apparent that it's not an ordinary flame." — search excerpt, likely from [ScreenRant: Studio Trigger Promare interview](https://screenrant.com/studio-trigger-promare-interview-fire-galo-kamina-ryuko/) or [ANN Anime Expo 2019 panel](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/convention/2019/anime-expo/promare-premiere-with-studio-trigger/.148866); unverified.
- **Promare shape symbolism (fan interpretation only):** a fan analysis says triangles represent Lio and the Burnish (rebellion), squares represent Galo and Kray (the system), and both merge into circles (harmony), with Trigger reportedly citing Zen. — [Privatter fan post](https://privatter.net/p/5326792). The official interviews I read (Ani-Gamers, Anime Herald) did not mention it, so treat it as unconfirmed.
- **Gunmen:** Gurren Lagann's face-torso mecha were designed by Imaishi and Yoh Yoshinari, and they copy their pilots' facial expressions. — [Gurren Lagann Wiki: Gunmen](https://gurrenlagann.fandom.com/wiki/Gunmen) (search excerpt)
- **Gotcha Force cast structure:** protagonist Kou's partner is G Red, and the villain group is the "Death Force". — [Gotcha Force Wiki](https://gotchaforce.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Tribes) (search excerpt)

### Inferences
**Core cast template.** Each human is paired with a partner mech whose archetype and colors echo theirs:

| Role | Personality | Color | Shape | Hair silhouette | Signature costume/prop | Partner mech |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero (kid protagonist) | Hot-blooded, friendship-powered (Red Oni) | Red + white/blue | Square body, triangle accents | Spiky, forward-swept, one oversized cowlick or ahoge | Goggles or headband (Digimon precedent), capsule launcher gauntlet | Hero-type, twin eyes + V-fin, red |
| Rival | Aloof, skilled, lone wolf (Blue Oni) | Blue/black + one accent | Triangle | Sleek, swept-back, or covering one eye | Long coat or scarf, gloves | Ninja, sniper or dark knight |
| Best friend / tech kid | Nerdy, analytical, comic | Green or yellow | Circle | Round or bowl cut; glasses as the silhouette hook | Laptop or scanner device, oversized backpack | Support/transformer or vehicle |
| Heroine (tomboy or idol) | Confident or bubbly | Pink/orange or cyan | Circle + triangle | Twin-tails, high ponytail or bob (avoid overlap with other girls) | Headset, jacket, custom capsule case | Magical-girl or idol mech |
| Big guy | Gentle strongman or bully-turned-ally | Yellow/brown | Square | Buzz cut or tall pompadour | Sports jersey, sneakers | Tank or brawler |
| Little sibling / mascot | Cheerful, gets kidnapped or saved | Pastel | Circle | Big round hair buns or oversized hat | Hat with ears | Mini-class pet mech |
| Mentor | Mysterious, masked or old | White/gray + gold | Square/triangle | White hair, or hidden by hat or mask | Mask, cape or lab coat; capsule-shop keeper | Samurai or wizard; a "legendary" mech |
| Rich-kid rival | Arrogant, buys rare capsules | Purple/gold | Triangle | Perfect coif or drill curls | Blazer, butler | Chrome/gold ultra-rare mech |
| Comic villain trio (grunts) | Bumbling | Matching drab uniforms with secondary colors | Mixed (tall/short/round silhouettes) | Distinct per member | Masks, uniforms | Mono-eye grunts |
| Villain lieutenant | Obsessive creator (the Akane role) | Purple + acid green or cream | Triangle | Striking, asymmetrical | Sculpting tools, sketchbook | Custom kaiju-toys she sculpts |
| Big bad | Cold, grand | Black + purple, or violet + icy blue ("cold technological terror") | Triangle/spike | Long flowing or none (helmet) | Throne, armor | Colossal kaiju or dark combiner |

**Design rules for the cast:**
1. Each character's hair and headwear silhouette must be unique across the whole cast, since hair is the main identifier.
2. Give each lead one "prop you could sell as a toy" (goggles, launcher, device).
3. Echo the partner mech's primary color and crest shape in the kid's outfit or hair (e.g., the hero's cowlick mirrors the V-fin), so pairs read together.
4. Color each child with one dominant hue, one neutral and one accent, matching the Sentai role colors.
5. Keep faces simple: big readable eyes, simple mouth shapes and minimal lines, so a procedural 3D/cel-shaded pipeline can swap expression decals.

**Expression sheet minimum** (general animation practice, not from a cited source): neutral, grin, determined, shouting/battle cry, shock, sad, smug, comic tears, sweat-drop exasperation, and a "capsule-opening excitement" face. For 3D, build eyes, brows and mouths as swappable texture decals or blend shapes.

**2000s Saturday-morning vibe vs modern trends:**
- **2000s** (Medabots, Beyblade, Yu-Gi-Oh, Digimon, Bakugan era):
  - bright primary costumes, spiky gravity-defying hair, goggles/headbands/fingerless gloves;
  - a "toy as partner" premise (V-pets, spinning tops, capsule balls);
  - a named elemental system;
  - bumbling villain trios, battle shouts, "monster-of-the-week" structure.
- **Modern Trigger/Gridman:**
  - flat color fields shared by characters and 3D mecha (Promare);
  - bold geometric shapes and neon off-palette accents (pink/green flames);
  - experiments with colored line art;
  - restraint in character features (Gridman's no-twintails rule, subtle mouth and gaze acting);
  - mecha and kaiju treated as physical suits/toys with limited articulation, which suits a toy-robot game.
  A successor can blend the 2000s premise, cast types and color-coding with the modern flat-color, geometric, "toy-as-physical-object" presentation.

**Gridman as thematic template:** a villain who sculpts or 3D-prints kaiju "toys" that come alive is a natural antagonist for a capsule-toy game. The villain's creations can be literal off-model bootleg capsule toys.

### Gaps

- The ANN SSSS.Gridman interview with Masaru Sakamoto and Masato Takeuchi returned 403, so character-design specifics beyond Amemiya's comments are missing.
- I found no authoritative source on expression-sheet conventions, so that list is general practice.

## 6. Villain and kaiju toy design: dark palettes, spikes, asymmetry, glowing eyes

### Takeaway
Villain and kaiju readability comes from inverting the hero code:
- mono-eye or hidden faces;
- secondary-color palettes (purple+green = scientific hubris, purple+orange = comic menace, purple+blue = cold technological terror);
- triangles and spikes;
- drab or dark values for grunts.

Kaiju should be abstracted hybrids of several animals or objects (Tohl Narita's rules): not simply enlarged real animals, not "spooks" with extra heads and limbs, and never gore. That also keeps them toy-friendly for children, matching Okawara's "avoid grotesque designs" principle.

### Cited Findings
- **Tohl Narita's three rules of kaiju design:**
  1. Kaiju are not "spooks": never a spook-like creature "with two heads and several limbs".
  2. Never a real creature that has simply "become huge in size".
  3. Nothing that evokes "the damaged body with the brain or internal organs exposed or with the blood running down".
  — [Ultopia: Three Rules of Kaiju Design by Tohl Narita](https://tokusatsujapan.com/kaiju-design-three-rules/) (search excerpt; page failed to load)
- Narita believed abnormal beings could only be created abstractly, with unusual proportions. Famous Ultra monsters combine traits of different animals; Gubila, for example, takes a drill horn from a narwhal and a body pattern from a clown triggerfish. Narita drew on the Greek idea of cosmos (order) for Ultraman, versus chaos for his Ultra Q monsters. — [TV Tropes: So You Want To Write the Next Ultraman](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/SoYouWantTo/WriteTheNextUltraman); [Easter's Kaiju Kompendium: Ultraman's Monsters Part 3](https://kaijukompendium.design.blog/ultramans-monsters-part-3/) (search excerpts)
- Narita's drawings showed "monsters transforming into buildings, stones and trees, and other inanimate objects", which is precedent for object-based kaiju. — [Wikipedia: Tohl Narita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohl_Narita)
- **Hero vs villain color theory (ComicsAlliance):** heroes typically wear primary colors (red, blue, yellow); villains wear secondaries (purple, green, orange). — [ComicsAlliance: Superhero Color Theory, Part II](https://comicsalliance.com/superhero-color-theory-part-ii-secondary-characters/)
  - Purple + green = "scientific hubris growing out of control" (Lex Luthor, Brainiac, Green Goblin, The Lizard).
  - Purple + orange = comic danger (Joker, Mr. Mxyzptlk, Batroc).
  - Purple + blue = "cold technological terrors" (Galactus, the Sentinels).
  - Exceptions: Aquaman's green/orange; the Hulk sends "the wrong signal" despite being sympathetic.
- **Disney villains:** purple + green is "a deadly pairing" that recurs across Disney villains. Purple reads as power, nobility and ambition, and green as envy. Villains tend toward darker or more muted tones. — [Regal: Disney Villains and the Psychology of Color](https://www.regmovies.com/movie-news/disney-villains-psychology-of-color); [Venngage: Disney villains color psychology](https://venngage.com/blog/disney-villains/); [Mattgyver: Disney's Color of Evil](https://www.mattgyver.com/tutorials/2022/12/26/disneys-color-of-evil) (search excerpts)
- **Mono-eye equals enemy (Zaku);** real-robot grunts are drab with blank, cycloptic or gas-mask heads (see Question 2). — [Zimmerit](https://www.zimmerit.moe/the-ms-06-zaku-ii-an-enduring-eye-con/); [TV Tropes: Real Robot Genre](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/RealRobotGenre)
- **Triangles** read as danger and cunning; the triangle-based design reads as "most threatening". — [Dream Farm Studios](https://dreamfarmstudios.com/blog/shape-language-in-character-design/); [CGWire](https://blog.cg-wire.com/character-shape-language/)
- **Gridman:** kaiju were rendered in 3DCG to avoid hand-drawn "strong character expressions", so viewers perceive them as environmental threats. — [Sakuga Blog](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2021/05/31/ssss-gridman-and-ssss-dynazenon-interview-series-director-akira-amemiya-on-the-franchises-production-and-style/)
- **Toy precedents for "dark" factions:**
  - Bakugan's Darkus attribute moved to purple and black. — [Bakugan Wiki: Darkus](https://bakugan.wiki/wiki/Darkus)
  - Duel Masters' Darkness civilization uses a black frame and a horned-mask icon. — [Duel Masters Wiki: Civilization](https://duelmasters.fandom.com/wiki/Civilization)
  - Gotcha Force had Death and Demon Borg tribes and a "Death Force" enemy faction. — [Gotcha Force Wiki](https://gotchaforce.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Tribes)
- **Okawara:** "I've always tried to avoid grotesque designs, striving for something enjoyable for children." — [Zeonic Republic](https://zeonic-republic.net/?page_id=11205)

### Inferences
**Villain mech toolkit (primitive recipes):**
- **Eyes:** a mono-eye in a slot rail (emissive sphere in a dark thin box), a jagged visor, or a hidden face with just two glowing slits. Use red, magenta or acid-green emissives. Glowing eyes also cut through dark arenas; this is a convention rather than a sourced rule.
- **Horns instead of a V-fin:** curved cones or bent tapered cylinders. A "broken V-fin" (one fin snapped) signals a corrupted hero.
- **Spikes:** cone arrays on shoulders, knuckles, spine and tail. Use them on silhouette edges so they register in the black-shape test.
- **Asymmetry:** one oversized claw, drill or cannon arm against a normal arm; one horn; one pauldron. Keep the legs symmetric so the figure still "stands firmly". Asymmetry was not covered by any source I found; this is standard concept-art practice.
- **Palettes:** purple + acid green (mad-scientist faction); purple + orange (comic villain trio); violet + ice blue or gunmetal (cold robotic empire); black + crimson (demon/final boss); desaturated olive/gray + pink mono-eye (grunts). Heroes keep primaries plus white, per ComicsAlliance.
- **Value:** keep villain bodies in a darker value band than heroes so the two sides separate even in grayscale (following TF2's value-and-hue readability approach).
- **Materials for evil variants:** smoked-translucent black plastic, oil-slick iridescence, glow-in-the-dark bone-white for undead, "corrupted" versions of hero mechs (same mesh, villain palette, red mono-eye swap). Corrupted versions are a cheap way to double roster value.

**Kaiju toy recipes following Narita's rules:**
- Combine 2–3 unrelated sources: animal + object + element. Examples: a narwhal-drill + triggerfish-pattern style pairing, lighthouse + crab, rice-cooker + turtle, traffic-cone + stegosaurus.
- Abstract the proportions: a huge head and tiny arms, or a tiny head on a huge body.
- Avoid extra heads and limbs, pure "giant real animal" designs, and gore.
- For undead or monster types, show a mechanical frame (Tinpet/bones) instead of organs, which stays within Narita's third rule and Okawara's no-grotesque rule while still reading as "skeletal".
- Make kaiju a size class above most mechs (Giant/Colossal), give them a single bold pattern (stripes, spots, a glowing chest core), and move them with deliberately limited, "suit-like" articulation, as Gridman did, so they feel like physical toys.
- **Narrative hook:** as in SSSS.Gridman, kaiju can be the villain's handmade toys. Visually they could be one-color, unpainted-looking, glossy "prototype sculpt" versions of kaiju that gain paint or glow as they power up.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced design guidance specific to asymmetry, spikes or glowing eyes in mecha/kaiju toys beyond general shape language and the mono-eye convention.
- I didn't research soft-vinyl (sofubi) kaiju toy conventions (typical colors, spray-paint accents, blind-bag kaiju lines).
- The primary Narita rules page (tokusatsujapan.com) failed to resolve, so the three rules come from its search excerpt. Wikipedia's Narita article doesn't list them.
